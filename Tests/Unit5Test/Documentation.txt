### *About Monster’s Elemental Type:*

Each Monster has its own Elemental Type.  When battling, this Monsters type is taken into account when calculating attack damage.   (For Example, if the Monster is FIRE type, it does additional damage with FIRE attacks).

### ***About Monster Stats:***

A Monster has integer values for its total health, attack, defense, and speed.

*When initializing a monster*, these stats can have any value, provided the total sum does not exceed 250. You may validate a monster's stats using `validateStats()` found in the `BattleEngine` class.

Note that if a Monster's stats are invalid, the `BattleEngine` ***must correct*** the issue ***but not throw*** an exception. The program crashing due to a "cheating" monster would not provide a pleasant experience for the players.

## Methods to code:

### About `attack(Monster opponent)` :

This method randomly selects an action:

- 45%: *performs Elemental Attack* using Montser’s `attackElementOne`
- 45%: *performs Elemental Attack* using Monster’s `attackElementTwo`
- 10%: *performs `DoubleDown` attack*

This method randomly determines which attack to carry out.  There are three possible actions: one of the monster's two `Elemental Attacks`, or its `Double Down` attack.   It then performs the correct attack.

Each `Elemental Attack` has a 45% chance of occurring. The remaining 10% chance is reserved for the `Double Down` attack.

The double down attack is only available once per battle.  

After using double down:

- 50%: *performs Elemental Attack* using Montser’s `attackElementOne`
- 50%: *performs Elemental Attack* using Monster’s `attackElementTwo`

### About `performElementalAttack(int attackNumber, Monster opponent)` :

This method carries out one of the two Monster’s attacks. 

***The implementation of this method is up to you.*** 

The damage calculation for each attack is as follows:

<aside>
🔥 $totalDamage =$

$baseDamage * (\frac{attack}{opponentDefense}) * STAB * effectivenessMultiplier * criticalMultiplier;$

</aside>

- The `$baseDamage$` is always set to 10, and is *different* from the `$attack$` stat value.
- The `$attack$` value is a value determined by the Monster’s attack attribute.
- The `$opponentDefense$` is the defense value of the opposing `Monster` object.
- The `$criticalMultiplier$` is `2.0` if the damage is critica.  Otherwise its `1.0`.
- The `$STAB$`, which stands for "Same-Type Attack Bonus", is a value of 1.5 if the attacking `Monster`'s element matches its `Elemental Attack` type. If they don't match, the value is 1.
- The `$effectivenessMultiplier$` is a value determined by **`getElementalMultiplier()`** found in the `ElementAttack` class. It’s value can be `2.0`, `1.0`, or `0.5`.

### About `performDoubleDownAttack(Monster opponent)` :

A `Double Down` attack is a special move that causes the monster to use both of their `Elemental Attacks` in a single turn. 

So a `Chimera` using `Double Down` would unleash its `*Pepper Breath*` and `*Deathly Scream*` attacks consecutively. 

Since each attack can critically strike individually, `Double Down` has the potential to deal massive amounts of damage! 

Due to its immense power, **this move can only be used once per battle**.  After the monster will only switch between the available two individual`Elemental Attacks`attacks.

## Elemental Effectiveness

<aside>
🚧

**`getElementalMultiplier(String attackElement, String monsterElement)`**

This method requires the attacking `Monster`’s `Elemental Attack` type and the defending `Monster`’s element type. 

It returns a value: 

- 2 for `Super Effectiveness`
- 1 for `Normal Effectiveness`

In this game, there are only 4 possible elements: Fire, Air, Earth, and Water.

## `Super Effectiveness`

When an element is stronger than another element, it’s known as `Super Effectiveness`.

Elemental `Super Effectiveness` is shown by the chart below:

Each element has a unique strength and weakness that can either increase or decrease the total damage dealt to a monster.

![download.svg](attachment:084f2703-7ed9-4b1b-b16a-0264957e61b4:download.svg)

`Fire -> Air -> Earth -> Water -> Fire`

An attack is considered `Super Effective` if it targets a monster with a particular weakness. 

For instance, Fire is stronger than Air, therefore a Fire `Elemental Attack` will inflict more damage when directed at an Air-type `Monster`. 

## `Low Effectiveness`

When an element is weaker than another element, it’s known as `Low Effectiveness`.

If the super effective chain goes in ***reverse*** then it displays `Low Effectiveness`: 

```
Fire <- Air <- Earth <- Water <- Fire <- Air ...
```

In this scenario, an Fire `Elemental Attack` is not very effective against a Water-type `Monster`, because Fire is weaker than Water. 

Another scenario of `Low Effectiveness` occurs when an `Elemental Attack` targets a monster *of the same element*. 

For instance, a Fire `Elemental Attack` targeting a Fire-type `Monster` will not be very effective. 

Similarly, Air is stronger than Earth, Earth is stronger than Water, and Water is stronger than Fire. 

- **Super effective:** `x2`
- **Low effective (reverse chain OR same type):** `x0.5`
- **Normal:** `x1`

## `Normal Effectiveness`

If a pair of elements are not directly connected in either the super effective or low effective chain, then the attack will deal its normal amount. There are no multiplier changes for this type of attack.

For example, Normal Effectiveness occurs when *Fire attacks Earth or Air attacks Water*.

<aside>
✅ **In general:**

**`Super Effective` attacks inflict *double* the damage `x2`.**

**`Low Effective` attacks inflict *half* the damage `x1/2`.**

**`Normal Effective`** **attacks inflict the regular amount of damage `x1`**

</aside>

<aside>
🚧 **`startBattle(Monster monster1, Monster monster2)`**

This method initiates a battle between two monsters. Each monster takes turns being an *attacker* and *defender*.

The speediest monster always attacks first in battle.  Tie needs to choose a RANDOM monster.

🚧 **`startBattle(ArrayList<Monster> monsters)`**

This method takes a list of monsters and initiates a tournament to determine the most dominant monster.

It begins by pitting the first two monsters against each other, determining the winner, and then matching that winner against the next monster in line.

The method returns the ultimate victor.

**Note:** Monsters at the end of the list have a higher probability of winning, which is acceptable. *A stretch challenge is available to make it fairer.*

</aside>

### `startBattle(Monster monster1, Monster monster2)`

Rules:

- invalid monster automatically loses (if both invalid, return `null` )
- faster monster goes first
    - (tie in speed: choose random first attacker)
- Monsters take turns calling `attack(.....)` against the other Monster
    - Alternate attacks until one health <= 0
    - Call `displayStatus(...)` after each attack
- Winner makes a victory noise!
- return winner

### `battleEveryone(ArrayList<Monster> monsters)`

Tournament rules:

- Monster 1 vs Monster 2
- Reset the health of the winner to the Monsters original max health and it makes its victory noise
- winner fights monster 3
- continue through list…
- return final winner
